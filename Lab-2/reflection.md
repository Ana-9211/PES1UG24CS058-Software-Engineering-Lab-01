**1. Did your estimations reflect the actual effort?**
Partly. The 8-point stories (FR-001 reservation, NFR-001 concurrency, NFR-002 access control) are the ones with the most rules to implement, and each has two tasks and sub-tasks. The 3-point story (FR-003 cancellation) was the smallest. Because the sprints were simulated, no real time was measured, so the estimates were not compared against actual effort.

**2. Was your backlog well-prioritized?**
Yes. The five High-priority stories (FR-001, FR-002, FR-004, NFR-001, NFR-002) went into Sprint 1 and the two Medium-priority ones (FR-003, FR-005) into Sprint 2. Reservation, calibration checks, concurrency and access control are what the system cannot work without. Cancellation and inventory editing depend on them.

**3. How did your simulated sprint align with your plan?**
Sprint 1 planned 34 points and completed 34 (everything Done). Sprint 2 planned 8 points. Both sprints were run on the same day, so the Sprint 2 stories were finished before its start date and Jira reports them as completed outside the sprint.

**4. What insights did the burndown chart give about your team's capacity?**
Sprint 1's burnup shows work completed in steps (8, then 16, 24, 29 and 34 points) and ending above the guideline line. Finishing everything in one sitting means the chart shows simulated progress, not real capacity. Sprint 1 carried 34 points against 8 in Sprint 2, so the load was unbalanced. A real team would set Sprint 1 below its demonstrated velocity.
